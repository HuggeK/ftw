"ftw": minor
---

An Apple Watch can charge the car and read the house without its phone (ADR 0009). The box keeps a second relay socket, `/r/<epoch>/<handle>/exchange`, beside its uplink, and answers a watch's one-shot HTTPS requests on it: a Noise IK handshake with prologue `ftw.exchange.v1:` that carries the watch's hello and the box's `hello_ok`, then `cmd` and `now`. Watch sessions live in a table of eight apart from the phones' four, so a watch never pushes a phone out, and each ends after four exchanges, a minute idle or three minutes. Every answer is two lane 0 frames, so a read, a command and a refusal have one length; a command's result that has not settled in 3 s leaves a tick in its place, and asking again with the same `cmdId` returns it. The box answers a command no sooner than 400 ms after it arrived, applied or refused, so the time to answer does not tell the relay which; a command that takes longer answers when it is done. The box takes at most 60 handshakes a minute and answers a stranger or a removed watch with silence. It now says `cmd.exchange`, so a phone can offer "Add watch". Losing the exchange socket changes nothing for the uplink or local control.
