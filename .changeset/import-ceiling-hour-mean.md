The import ceiling now caps the mean grid import over each local clock hour, as a power tariff bills it, instead of every second. Only the site meter counts toward the hour's budget, and a restart keeps what the hour has already imported. The EV and battery are cut only when the hour's budget needs it, so a short house peak no longer throttles the car, and a quiet start to the hour lets it charge above the ceiling later in that hour. Increases wait for a step of room, at most once a minute, five minutes after a cut and not in the hour's last quarter, so the charger does not hunt. One reading over the ceiling no longer stops a car: an overage smaller than one charging step must last 20 seconds before the hour's budget cuts. A stopped car restarts when the house's mean load over the restart wait leaves room for its minimum under the ceiling itself, so it never starts on one hour's slack only to stop when the next hour begins. A ceiling set during an hour counts from then. A meter gap never makes room. The physical fuse stays an instant limit. `/api/status` shows the hour's budget left (`peak_import_hour_left_wh`) and the import limit in force (`peak_import_limit_w`).
